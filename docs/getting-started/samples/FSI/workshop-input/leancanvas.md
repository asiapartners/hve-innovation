## Lean Canvas

| Canvas area | Draft workshop input | Evidence state |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Relationship managers may spend substantial time assembling client context from disconnected systems before meetings. Fragmented information may cause inconsistent preparation, missed opportunities, and avoidable compliance checks. | Assumption |
| Target customer segments | Potential users include relationship managers and their team leads in commercial banking, private banking, wealth management, or insurance. Potential buyers include business-unit leaders, operations leaders, and technology sponsors. The primary segment is unknown. | Assumption |
| Value proposition | Help relationship managers prepare for client conversations by bringing approved client context, relevant policies, and suggested follow-up actions into one governed workflow while retaining human decision authority. | Assumption |
| Core offering | A relationship-manager assistant that produces client briefings, retrieves approved information, highlights possible risks or opportunities, and drafts follow-up actions for human review. The minimum viable workflow is not yet confirmed. | Assumption |
| Channels | Possible channels include an internal Microsoft 365 experience, an existing CRM surface, direct enterprise deployment, Microsoft Marketplace, and partner-led delivery. The preferred acquisition and delivery channels are unknown. | Assumption |
| Revenue model | Possible models include per-user subscription, enterprise subscription, managed-service fees, or bundled professional services. Pricing, billable units, willingness to pay, and Marketplace transaction design are unknown. | Unknown |
| Cost structure | Likely cost areas include product development, approved data integration, identity and access management, AI consumption, monitoring, security and compliance review, support, and customer onboarding. Actual costs are unknown. | Assumption |
| Success metrics | Candidate measures include preparation time saved, briefing completeness, active-user adoption, accepted suggested actions, portfolio coverage, compliance exceptions, and relationship-manager satisfaction. Baselines and targets are unknown. | Assumption |
| Unique advantage | Potential differentiation may come from FSI-specific workflows, governed access to enterprise context, policy-aware responses, traceability, and integration into existing relationship-manager tools. No durable advantage has been validated. | Assumption |

## Evidence-backed facts

* The workshop topic is a Relationship Manager Intelligence Assistant for FSI.
* Only synthetic or public material in the approved workshop evidence location may
  be used without additional human approval.
* The solution scope, users, business rules, constraints, AI guardrails, and
  publication assumptions still require evidence.

## Unknowns for follow-up

1. Which FSI segment and relationship-manager role should the MVP serve first?
2. What current workflow, delay, error, or missed outcome creates the highest
   business impact?
3. Which systems and approved information sources are used in that workflow?
4. Which actions must remain human decisions, and which policies govern them?
5. Who owns the budget and purchasing decision?
6. Which delivery channel fits the users' current workflow?
7. What commercial model and unit of value will customers accept?
8. What baseline and target will demonstrate an effective MVP?
9. Which alternatives already address the problem, including process changes
   without AI?
10. What evidence supports a defensible advantage over those alternatives?

## Workshop validation

The SME should validate the problem and affected users first. The PM should not
convert pricing, success measures, or delivery channels into requirements until
supporting evidence or an accountable decision owner confirms them.
