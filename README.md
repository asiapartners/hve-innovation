
## Hypervelocity Innovation
### From Innovation to Marketplace, Accelerated

Hypervelocity Innovation is an end-to-end way of working that helps teams turn research and discovery into valuable solutions, Marketplace and Copilot Agentic IP monetization, and publication with speed, quality, and security.

Design Thinking enables innovation by uncovering real human needs, reframing meaningful problems, and creating space to explore possibilities beyond familiar answers. By shaping and testing solutions with users, it turns insight into outcomes that are desirable, feasible, and valuable. Hypervelocity Engineering(HVE) accelerates that work by connecting discovery to requirements, planning, implementation, and review without losing the evidence or intent along the way.

Use this repository when you want to run that repeatable innovation cycle with product thinking, design thinking, engineering execution, AI-assisted delivery, to monetizing your innovation on Microsoft Marketplace, or Copilot Agent Store.

## How HVE Accelerates Innovation

HVE is the acceleration layer inside Hypervelocity Innovation. It helps teams move quickly while preserving the quality needed to build solutions people can use, organizations can operate, and teams can publish or monetize responsibly.

It connects:

* Research-first decision quality
* AI Discovery and Design Thinking centered on real user needs
* Structured requirements (BRD and PRD)
* Milestone planning and backlog creation
* Iterative implementation and review loops
* Publication readiness for your solution

HVE Core provides the tools, prompts, agents, and validation conventions to execute this innovation cycle consistently.

Innovation accelerates when teams reduce handoff loss and decision ambiguity while keeping users and outcomes at the center.

HVE speeds the path from insight to impact by:

* Turning discovery outputs into implementation-ready artifacts
* Keeping every milestone grounded in evidence through RPI (Research, Plan, Implement, Review)
* Aligning product, design, architecture, and engineering around the same artifact trail
* Reusing proven templates and automation instead of rebuilding process each time

## Getting Started with Hypervelocity Innovation

The [Hypervelocity Innovation Partner Workshop](docs/getting-started/partner-workshop.md) introduces a team from a shared problem to a connected set of requirements, backlog, architecture, implementation, and publication artifacts.

1. Overview
  * Agree on the outcome, sample scenario or customer problem, team roles, and the artifacts the team will create.
2. Shared setup
  * Complete [Partner Workshop Setup](docs/getting-started/partner-workshop-setup.md) so the team can work in one project with HVE Core and GitHub Copilot.
3. Role tracks: research, plan, envision, experience, architecture design, and backlog
  * Start with research and domain context, then frame the opportunity and desired outcome.
  * Use design thinking and user research, including AI Discovery Cards activities, to understand needs and shape the experience.
  * Create the BRD and PRD direction, identify acceptance criteria, and translate the solution into a prioritized backlog.
  * Develop the technical architecture and publication considerations in parallel with the emerging product direction.
  * Follow [Partner Workshop Role Tracks](docs/getting-started/partner-workshop-role-tracks.md) and connect design outputs to engineering with [DT to RPI Integration](docs/design-thinking/dt-rpi-integration.md).
4. Validation and solution integration
  * Bring the role artifacts together, resolve contradictions, review traceability, identify risks, and agree on the first implementation scope in [Partner Workshop Solution](docs/getting-started/partner-workshop-solution.md).
5. Marketplace and Copilot Agent Store readiness
  * Define the product, ownership, integration contract, commercial path, and unresolved gates in [Partner Workshop Publishing](docs/getting-started/partner-workshop-publishing.md).
6. Implementation and commercialization
  * Turn the approved workshop outputs into an evidence-based delivery plan, execute with RPI, and prepare the path to implementation and IP monetization in [Partner Workshop Implementation](docs/getting-started/partner-workshop-implementation.md).

The workshop is the front door to the wider operating model. After the session, use the [RPI Workflow](docs/rpi/README.md) to research remaining gaps, plan a narrow milestone, implement it, and review the evidence before expanding scope.

## Team Readiness and Change Management

Hypervelocity Innovation changes how teams make decisions, record context, and move from ideas to delivery. Treat adoption as a change-management effort, not only a tooling rollout.

### HVE Adoption Considerations

* **Visible sponsorship:** An accountable sponsor protects time, resolves priority conflicts, and accepts the first milestone so the workshop leads to delivery.
* **Complete perspectives:** Include the business owner, subject matter expert, designer, and technical owner so the team can make well-informed decisions together.
* **Clear decision rights:** Name who approves the problem framing, requirements, architecture direction, backlog priority, and publication path.
* **One repository, different audiences:** Non-technical and technical participants can work in the same repository when readable workshop artifacts are separated from implementation code, folders and templates are clear, and review permissions are agreed. Markdown documents, diagrams, requirements, and backlog plans should be approachable to all participants; source code and deployment changes should use normal engineering review gates.
* **Focused engineering involvement:** Keep the core workshop group small while bringing additional engineers and specialists into targeted reviews as the solution becomes clearer. Adding more engineers can reduce velocity when coordination, conflicting approaches, and communication gaps outweigh the work being delivered. Preserve momentum with clear ownership, small working groups, shared decisions, short feedback loops, and an agreed path for resolving conflicts.
* **Owned follow-up:** Assign an owner and next review date to every unresolved assumption, decision, publication gate, and backlog item.

### Recommended Team

Start with **four to six core people** for the workshop and first solution scope. One person may cover more than one role in a smaller team, but each responsibility must still be explicit.

| Role | Primary responsibility |
|------|------------------------|
| Sponsor or accountable product owner | Sets the outcome, protects capacity, resolves priority conflicts, and approves the first milestone |
| Facilitator or project manager | Runs the workshop sequence, maintains decisions, coordinates the backlog, and tracks follow-up |
| Subject matter expert | Supplies domain facts, user context, constraints, terminology, and evidence |
| Designer or researcher | Leads user research, design thinking, AI Discovery Cards, journey, accessibility, and experience decisions |
| Technical lead or architect | Frames the solution, architecture, security, integration, and operational constraints |
| Engineer or forward-deployed builder | Tests feasibility, sizes the first implementation slice, and turns the approved plan into working software |

For a small idea team, combine sponsor and project manager, or combine technical lead and engineer. Add security, privacy, data, platform, finance, legal, or commercialization specialists as **part-time reviewers** when the decision requires them. Do not make every specialist a permanent workshop participant.

The goal is a team large enough to represent the problem and delivery environment, but small enough to make decisions in the room. Use the [Partner Workshop Role Tracks](docs/getting-started/partner-workshop-role-tracks.md) to assign ownership before the session and the [Partner Workshop Solution](docs/getting-started/partner-workshop-solution.md) to confirm gaps before implementation.

## Rapid Iterative Solution Delivery

The expected operating pattern is rapid, milestone-based delivery:

* Research to reduce uncertainty
* Plan a narrow scope for each milestone
* Implement with explicit quality gates
* Review and route follow-up

Run this repeatedly with [RPI Workflow](docs/rpi/README.md) and scale using [Partner Workshop Solution](docs/getting-started/partner-workshop-solution.md).
